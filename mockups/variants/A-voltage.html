Panels almost never fail. Wiring does. Whichever column you end up in, the part that decides whether a system is safe in ten years is the part nobody photographs: correct cable sizing, real breakers, surge protection, and a DC isolator you can actually reach. It is also the easiest place for a cheap quote to save money without you noticing.

The thing most installers do not tell you before you sign. A plain grid-tied system switches itself off during a brownout, even in full sunlight. That is required by law, so that no power flows back into a line a lineman is working on. If you want the lights to stay on when the grid goes down, you need a battery, and that is the hybrid column.

Services offered

What we do for you.

Many companies sell you the panels and disappear. Here one team does the design, the build, the net metering and the maintenance. We have looked after a 489 kWp mall roof since 2023.

01

Solar plant system design and integration

Load study, shade study, string layout, single line diagram, structural check. Drawn against your real bills and your real roof before anything is ordered.

02

Engineering, procurement and construction

We engineer it, buy the equipment, build it and switch it on. Net metering can be filed by you, or we can process it for you. One contract, one team to answer for it.

03

Solar plant operation and maintenance

For plants that have to keep earning. Production monitoring, fault response, inverter servicing and written reports.

04

Solar plant preventive maintenance

Scheduled washing, thermal and electrical checks, bolt torque checks, and a performance report. Dust and salt air cost more output than most owners expect.

Panels are rated for the wind. What fails in a storm is the mounting, usually because someone bolted rails to purlins nobody checked. We calculate the uplift for your roof, use anchors rated above it, and show you those numbers in the proposal.

Only if you have a battery. A plain grid-tied system shuts off the moment the grid goes down, even in full sun. That is the law, so nothing flows back into a line someone is repairing. Production drops by roughly a third in July and August. It never goes to zero, because panels run on daylight, not direct sun. We size against your twelve month bill history, so the wet months are already in the numbers.
