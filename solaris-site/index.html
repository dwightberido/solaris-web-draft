Panels almost never fail. Wiring does.Whichever system you end up with, the part that decides whether it is safe in ten years is the part nobody photographs: cable sizing, real breakers, surge protection, and a DC isolator you can reach. It is also the easiest place for a cheap quote to save money without you noticing.
What most installers do not say before you sign.A plain grid-tied system switches itself off during a brownout, even in full sun. That is the law, so no power flows back into a line a lineman is working on. If you want the lights on when the grid goes down, you need a battery. That is the hybrid.

Panels are rated for the wind. What fails in a storm is the mounting, usually because someone bolted rails to purlins nobody checked. We calculate the uplift for your roof, use anchors rated above it, and show you those numbers in the proposal. Storm damage itself is not covered by the warranty.

Only if you have a battery. A plain grid-tied system shuts off the moment the grid goes down, even in full sun. That is the law, so nothing flows back into a line someone is repairing. Production drops by roughly a third in July and August. It never goes to zero, because panels run on daylight, not direct sun. We size against your twelve month bill history, so the wet months are already in the numbers.
